Sun Board V1 Design Notes

This board is designed to distribute power and supervise up to 5 Planet Boards by communicating via UART.
It has an SPST switch to power ON/OFF the whole robot.

Power Supply
The maximum voltage in the robot is 12.6V (LiPo 3S battery), so all the components on the power rail are rated for 25V or more.

The maximum current pulled by the robot is around 8A (considering that all the motors are stalling, which shouldn't happen and some software protections will be used). 
The Sun Board can handle up to 15A on the power rail, 2A for the 5V rail (RPI) and 2A for the 3.3V rail.

The eFuse used is a TPS259827, with the following configuration:
Undervoltage protection at 11V to protect the battery
Circuit Breaker at 15A
Current monitoring giving analog value from 0 to 2.97V (0 to 15A)
Lowest slew rate (0.68V/ms)

STM32
The model used is a STM32G031G8U6, same as the Planet Boards to keep the BOM consistent.
The specifications of this model has are : 
6 timers
2 SPI bus
2 UART bus
Real-Time Clock
28 pins including 26 GPIO and 15 ADC channels
14 External Interrupts (not on all pins) with 4 levels
Low-power modes
64 MHz CPU frequency

A useful function of STM32s here is that they can program, reset and boot each other via UART.

The Sun Board still includes a 4-pin header to program via SWD

Finally, some pins are compatible with 5V (reception only), which are nice to make the architecture even more modular.

Planet Board Connexion
The Sun Board is connected to the Planet Boards via 5 wires :
- 3.3V
- VCC
- GND
- TX
- RX

Since each Planet Board can pull up to 2A, the connectors and wires are sized for a 3A constant load.

The Sun Board's STM32 communicates with the Planet Boards' STM32s via UART.
Only one resistor is used on the Sun Board to keep a modular structure and since the architecture is closer to a Star topology than a Chain one 

BNO085
To balance the robot, a BNO085 is used as is has almost no drift.
The communication to the STM32 is done via SPI bus.

RPI Zero 2 W
The RPI handles the communication to the computer and camera feedback.
It rests on a female pin header and M2 standoffs and communicates with the STM32 via UART.
